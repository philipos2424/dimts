Never lose a quote you agreed on the phone

Most jobs are priced on a call from the van. Dimts records the call with a consent notice and writes down the price, the extras, the start date and who promised what next.

Where the money leaks

  • The customer remembers "$4,000 all in"; you remember "$4,000 plus materials".
  • An extra agreed on the phone never makes it onto the invoice.
  • You promised a revised quote by Thursday, and by Thursday they've called someone else.
  • A supplier's "delivered Monday" turns into Wednesday, and nobody wrote down who said Monday.

What Dimts gives you after each call

Agreed: the price, quantities, extras and dates, each with a link to the second it was said. When someone says "I never agreed to that", you can play it.

Promises, per customer: on pink, what you owe (the quote, the call-back). On yellow, what you're waiting on (the deposit, the site access code). They stay on the customer's page across every call until they're done.

A drafted follow-up: a short email confirming the price and the next step, ready to review and send. That email is often the difference between winning and losing the job.

No new phone and nothing for the customer to install. Dimts rings your own phone, then connects the customer. They hear a short recording notice before anyone speaks.
