Business call recording laws: UK, EU, Canada and the Gulf

The rules differ by country and, in the US, by state. One habit keeps you on the right side of almost all of them: tell everyone on the call that it's recorded, before the conversation starts.

Updated October 2026. This is a plain-language overview, not legal advice. Check the rules where you and the people you call are.

One-party and all-party consent

In a one-party consent place, you can record a call you take part in without telling the other person. In an all-party consent place, everyone on the call has to agree. When the two people are in different places, the stricter rule is the safe one to follow.

United States

Federal law and most states are one-party consent; about a dozen states, including California, Florida and Pennsylvania, require everyone's consent. See the full list in our guide to call recording laws by state.

Canada

The Criminal Code allows recording a conversation you take part in. Businesses are also covered by privacy law (PIPEDA and provincial equivalents), which expects you to tell people why you're recording and what you'll do with it.

United Kingdom

Businesses may record calls for legitimate reasons, such as keeping a record of what was agreed. The Investigatory Powers Act and the Telecommunications (Lawful Business Practice) Regulations allow it for business purposes, and UK GDPR expects you to tell people the call is recorded and why. A notice at the start of the call is the usual way.

European Union

GDPR requires a lawful basis and a clear notice. Some countries go further: in Germany, recording a private conversation without consent is a criminal offence, and Austria is similarly strict. There, get an explicit yes before recording.

The Gulf: UAE and Saudi Arabia

The UAE, Saudi Arabia and their neighbours treat recording someone without permission as a serious offence, with fines and possible prison under UAE privacy and cybercrime law. Always get explicit agreement before recording, not just a notice.

The simple rule that works everywhere

  1. Tell everyone the call is recorded before anyone speaks.
  2. Where the law asks for it, get an explicit yes, not just silence.
  3. Say what the recording is for, keep it secure, and delete it when you no longer need it.
  4. Let people ask for their data to be deleted.
